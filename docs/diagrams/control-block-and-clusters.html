§1 The base control-block diagram

One follower vehicle i, its predecessor i−1. Two genuinely separate signals arrive from the predecessor — a physical radar sighting (bottom, feedback) and a radioed acceleration value (top, feedforward) — and that separation is the entire mechanism CACC is built on. +/− mark the summing junction's two inputs; dashed = an internal tap, not an external sensor.

Reading this diagram: the summing junction computes e_i = d_i − d_des,i (T-02) — actual gap in on +, the speed-dependent desired gap in on −. The controller is drawn with its internal summing block exposed because u_i genuinely is three terms added together, one of which (ka·u_ff_eff) comes from a completely different sensing modality than the other two. The dashed loop is not a sensor reading — it's the follower's own speedometer, feeding its own reference generator; nothing about it depends on the predecessor at all. Saturation lives inside the Actuator block, which is why the certification suite checks it on the raw nonlinear simulation separately from the linear ‖H̃‖∞ theory (T-01, T-09 §1).

I

Core Engine

Build the one shared, honest foundation everything else stands on: how a car moves, how it decides what to do, how many cars run forward in time together, and the one true mathematical judge of "is this safe."

SYS 1
Vehicle model
double integrator + actuator lag — engines/brakes don't respond instantly
SYS 2
Control-law bank
three drivers (ACC / CACC / CTHP) for a fair comparison
SYS 3
Platoon orchestrator
the conductor — steps every car forward together, predecessor-only visibility
SYS 4
Stability analyzer
the referee — the one exact ‖H̃‖∞ safety verdict everyone reports through
II

The V2V Channel

Be honest that a wireless link isn't a wire: it can lie a little, arrive late, or not arrive at all — three separate failure modes, modeled separately.

SYS 5
Noise model
the radio rounds/compresses the value it sends (16-bit)
SYS 6
Delay model
a message isn't visible until it physically arrives
SYS 7
Packet-loss model
sometimes it just never arrives — found: delay hurts far more than loss
III

QoS-Adaptive Pipeline

Give the car a sixth sense about its own radio, instead of assuming a perfect, known channel. The project's first original contribution.

SYS 8
Channel estimator
"how good is my radio right now" — beacon vs. radar mismatch
SYS 9
Timestamp predictor
un-delays the message using its own timestamp
SYS 10
Headway adapter
smoothly widens/closes the gap on what's now known
IV

Gain Adaptation

When widening the gap alone isn't enough, also change how hard the car reacts — not just how far back it sits.

SYS 11
Online gain scheduler
moves kp/kv live too, breaking the fixed-gain "wall" System 10 hits alone
V

Metrics & Certification

Turn a pile of simulation numbers into a decision a human can trust without reading code.

SYS 12
Metrics & logging
L2 amplification, throughput, RMS accel — deterministic, reproducible
SYS 13
Certification harness
one command, a fixed scenario matrix, a pass/fail report — the "test track" button
VI

ROS 2 Distributed Backend

Stop taking the easy way out — prove the control law survives as separate real processes with real clocks, not one convenient shared-memory script.

SYS 14
Message package
the shared wire format every node speaks
SYS 15
Per-vehicle node
one real OS process per car, imports Sys 1–2 verbatim
SYS 16
Distributed V2V node
Sys 5–7's impairments as real message-passing
SYS 17
Leader & recorder
the prescribed maneuver + the CSV logger
VII

Visualization

Make the trustworthy math actually visible — different audiences need genuinely different views.

SYS 18
rviz2 3-D markers
live, color-coded, camera rides with the platoon
SYS 19
Gazebo world
a real physics engine, driven by our physics, not its own
SYS 20
Offline renderer
top-down MP4/GIF from any run, no ROS needed
SYS 21
Browser demo
the real physics in JS, live sliders, zero backend
SYS 22
Results dashboard
auto-discovers every run, one self-contained HTML report
VIII

Flagship

Go past "deploy the paper honestly" — defend against three failure modes the base paper never poses as questions at all: being late, being lied to, and not knowing how safe "safe" really is.

SYS 23
Predictive QoS map
opens the gap before a mapped bad-radio zone, not after
SYS 24
Trust gate
caps how much a lying radio can inject — provable bound
SYS 25
Risk certificate
turns pass/fail into an exact failure probability
